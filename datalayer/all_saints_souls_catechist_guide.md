## p. 237 — ENGAGE

**Outcomes**
- Understand that All Saints Day and All Souls Day are celebrations of the Communion of Saints.
- Explain that in Christ, we are united with the living and the dead.
- Discuss the saints as examples of how we can live our lives.

**Student page: All Saints Day and All Souls Day**
> We celebrate All Saints Day on November 1 by remembering the holy people who now live with God in heaven. We sing to each of the saints by name, asking them to pray to God for us. Filled with their holiness, we follow their examples.
>
> We celebrate All Souls Day on November 2 by remembering those who have died but whose souls are being purified in Purgatory to live with God forever.

Prayer: *Jesus, let me be united with you and with all the angels and saints, and with my brothers and sisters.*

Photo: girl praying in front of a Día de los Muertos ofrenda (candles, photo, sugar skulls, papel picado).

**1 Set the Stage.** Write *All Hallows' Eve* on the board. Say: Remember that *hallowed* means holy. Ask: What do you think *hallows* means? (those who are hallowed — that is, holy people) Say: The hallows are the saints. We call October 31 "All Hallows Eve" because it is the eve of, or day before, All Saints Day, which is November 1. This is where the name Halloween comes from. Say: In this session, we will learn that the saints dedicated their lives to serving God and others, following Jesus' example.

**2 Get Started.** Invite volunteers to read aloud the title and the two paragraphs. Explain that souls in Purgatory are being cleansed of sin so that they can experience fully the joy of heaven.

**Prayer.** Invite children to sit quietly and prepare for prayer. Say: Let us ask Jesus to help us serve God and others as the saints did. Pray the prayer. Pause as children reflect. Then pray *Amen*.

**If Time Allows — Watch** a video about the saints (QR code).

**Get Ready for the Session.** Bibles, basic writing and art supplies, a small statue of Mary, small heart stickers. Check any If Time Allows activities for extra materials.

## p. 238 — EXPLORE

**Student page: Like Saints**
> We become holy like the saints when we love as Jesus did. The Holy Spirit gives us the grace to do so. In the New Testament, John's letter tells us God's love grows in us when we love.
>
> *Beloved, if God so loved us, we also must love one another. No one has ever seen God. Yet, if we love one another, God remains in us, and his love is brought to perfection in us. This is how we know that we remain in him and he in us, that he has given us of his Spirit.* — 1 John 4:11–13
>
> One way the Holy Spirit gives us the grace to love is through the sacraments. We can show God's love through kindness. Kindness means treating others as people loved by God.

> **The Communion of Saints.** We are not alone! We are all together in Jesus. All who love God, including the living, those who have died and are being purified, and those who are blessed in heaven, together form the Communion of Saints. When we pray, even if for a need of our own, our prayers affect all who follow Jesus.

Portraits: Saint Teresa Benedicta of the Cross · Venerable Pierre Toussaint · Saint Thérèse of Lisieux · Blessed Carlos Rodríguez. (Audio QR code.)

**1 Begin.** Invite children to share times when they have shown love to someone, such as helping a brother tie his shoe or talking to a lonely or elderly neighbor. Say: When we serve others, God's love grows in us.

**2 Connect.** Direct attention to the four portraits. Say: These are pictures of four people from the past 200 years who have been named holy by the Church. They devoted their lives to serving God and others. Have children point to each picture as you read the names aloud together.

Invite a volunteer to read the first paragraph and the Scripture. Ask: What happens when we love one another? (God's love grows in us and in others.) Why does John say that no one has ever seen God? (It makes it even more amazing that we feel his love so strongly.) How do we know we are in God and he is in us? (through the Holy Spirit)

Invite a volunteer to read The Communion of Saints. Ask: Where else have we heard the word *communion*? (as a name for the Eucharist) Say: Communion means togetherness — coming together as one. The Eucharist brings us together in Jesus Christ. Ask: Who is together in the Communion of Saints? (everyone, living and dead, who loves God) Say: The Communion of Saints doesn't just include the saints in heaven. It includes you, me, and everyone who follows Jesus.

## p. 239 — EXPLORE

**Student page: Saints in Heaven and on Earth**
> When we celebrate All Saints Day and All Souls Day, we remember all who are with God in heaven and all who are awaiting heaven in Purgatory. We remember that as followers of Jesus on earth, we are united in Jesus Christ because we are part of the Communion of Saints.

**ACTIVITY: A Saint in the Making.** We live as saints when we recognize God's love in each person. On the lines, write a story in which you confirm someone's dignity. It can be a made-up story or something that really happened. (Answers will vary.)

Wraparound: Read aloud Saints in Heaven and on Earth. Ask: What do we celebrate on All Saints Day and All Souls Day? (those who have died and are with God in heaven, those awaiting heaven in Purgatory, and the followers of Jesus on earth) To explain Purgatory, say: Our sins separate us from God and others. When people die, they may not have asked forgiveness for all their sins, and there may still be a separation between them and God. Before people can see God face to face in heaven, this separation must first be removed. We call this experience Purgatory. Those in Purgatory are being purified in preparation for meeting God face to face. Say: In their lives, the saints knew God's love for each person, and they treated everyone they met with dignity.

ACTIVITY: Invite a volunteer to read A Saint in the Making. Ask what it means to confirm someone's dignity. (It means never to treat any person in a way that is hurtful or scornful.) Allow time to complete the stories.

**3 Close.** Say: We are all saints whenever we treat someone in a way that shows that we know God loves them. Be sure to pray often that you always will do that. Let's keep these ideas in mind as we try to live like saints every day.

**If Time Allows — My Example.** Each child picks a saint to follow and writes a paragraph on why. Tell them about your favorite saint as an example. Volunteers read aloud.

## p. 240 — REFLECT / RESPOND

**Prayer Service** (setup). Prayer table with a Bible open to 1 Corinthians 12:12–13 and a statue or picture of Mary. Pick a volunteer as Reader. Point out the *All* parts. Catechist is Leader. Say: As we pray, we look to Mary, our greatest example of a saint, who is sometimes called "Queen of All Saints." Pray the service, then the Hail Mary together. Close with the song "Holy Is Your Name" (QR code), then return quietly to places.

> **Leader:** Let us praise our loving God. Blessed be God forever.
> **All:** Blessed be God forever.
> **Leader:** Jesus is our example of God's love for us. We pray that in everything we do, we will follow him and become holy.
> **Reader:** A reading from the first Letter of Paul to the Corinthians.
> *As a body is one though it has many parts, and all the parts of the body, though many, are one body, so also Christ. For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, slaves or free persons, and we were all given to drink of one Spirit.* — 1 Corinthians 12:12–13
> The Word of the Lord.
> **All:** Thanks be to God.
> **Leader:** We ask the saints in heaven to pray to God for us. Let us close by asking Mary to pray for us.

**1 Respond.** Children share three new facts or ideas about All Saints Day and All Souls Day. Say: These feasts are a beautiful reminder that we are not alone — we are part of the Communion of Saints!

**2 Go in Peace.** Collect materials. Remind children to share their activities with their families. Put a small heart sticker on each child's hand. Say: Whenever you look at your sticker, remember the example of the saints. Try to follow their example by living to serve others.

**If Time Allows — Group Reports.** Four groups, one question each: How can we be like the saints? Who is in the Communion of Saints? When in our lives can we be like the saints? Whom do we remember on All Saints Day?

**Coaching Children to Pray.** Assure children that we can always ask the saints in heaven to pray for us and for our loved ones who have died. Those loved ones are with us in a special way in the Communion of Saints.
