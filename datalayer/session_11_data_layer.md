# Teaching Guide Data Layer — Grade 3 Finding God

This file contains the full teaching material for two related sessions:
1. **Session 11: Jesus Sends the Holy Spirit** (Unit 3, pp. 89–96) — the main weekly session
2. **Pentecost** (The Year in Our Church, pp. 233–236) — the liturgical-feast session

Both sessions share the same core theme (the Holy Spirit) but approach it differently. Session 11 tells the Pentecost *story* and teaches the Fruits of the Holy Spirit. The Pentecost feast pages focus on *gifts* of the Holy Spirit and each person's unique calling.

# SESSION 11: JESUS SENDS THE HOLY SPIRIT

## Session Metadata

- **Session number:** 11
- **Unit:** 3 — *The Church, Our Community in the Spirit*
- **Session theme:** Jesus sends the Holy Spirit to bring life to the Church.
- **Approximate total time:** 60 minutes (broken into four steps)
- **Pages in teacher guide:** 89–96
- **Primary Scripture:** John 14:15–21 (Jesus promises the Advocate / Spirit of truth)
- **Secondary Scripture:** Galatians 5:22–23 (Fruits of the Spirit)
- **Key vocabulary word:** *witness* — someone who tells others about Jesus

## Session Outcomes (what children should be able to do by the end)

1. Discuss Jesus' promise of the Holy Spirit to the Apostles.
2. Tell the story of the Holy Spirit coming to the disciples at Pentecost.
3. Describe how the Holy Spirit helps us live prayerful lives.
4. Discuss the Fruits of the Holy Spirit.
5. Define *witness*.

## STEP 1 — ENGAGE (p. 89) — 15 minutes

### Page header: "Jesus Sends the Holy Spirit"

### Student-facing content on p. 89 (God's Word summary)

**Read-aloud/listen cue:** John 14:15–21

Summary text for the student:
> When Jesus sat down with the disciples at the Last Supper, he knew they would not see him again until after his Resurrection. But he told them he would be with them always in their minds and hearts. He promised there always would be someone to fill them with strength and love, which he called "the Spirit of truth." At Pentecost, this Spirit came to the disciples as the Holy Spirit. Through the Holy Spirit, the Apostles were able to bring many people to Jesus.
>
> The Holy Spirit lives in us too and is with us always. Through the Holy Spirit, we receive the help we need to serve God and share Jesus' love with everyone we meet.

### Opening Prayer (student page)

> Jesus, open my heart to your commandment that we love one another. Help me know the Spirit of truth, which is the Holy Spirit.

### 1. Open Your Heart
Invite children to quiet themselves and prepare to hear sacred Scripture.

### 2. Proclaim God's Word
- Take the Bible from the Prayer Center.
- Read **John 14:15–21** aloud.
- Then **say:** *"The Gospel of the Lord."*
- Invite children to respond: *"Praise to you, Lord Jesus Christ."*

### 3. The Word Alive (discussion script)

Ask if anyone has had a friend who stood up for them.

**Say:** *"As I read, think about how reassuring it is to know that someone will watch out for you."*

Read the paragraphs aloud.

**Say:** *"Now think about how reassuring it is to know that Jesus sends the Holy Spirit to help and guide you every day."*

**Ask:** *"How does the Holy Spirit help us?"*
- **Expected answer:** He makes sure we are not alone but always have Jesus' love with us.

### Closing Prayer for Step 1

**Say:** *"Let's ask Jesus to help us receive the gift of the Holy Spirit."*

Pray aloud the prayer from the student book. Conclude by praying *Amen*.

### Understanding Scripture (catechist background — do not read to class)

In John's Gospel, Jesus gives a long talk to his disciples at the Last Supper. He is saying good-bye to them, although the disciples do not completely comprehend this. Here Jesus says that as long as they keep his commandment — to love one another — the disciples will have an "Advocate" with them, which Jesus calls "the Spirit of truth." Furthermore, Jesus promises to be present, after he departs, to whoever loves him. The disciples will find this out when the Holy Spirit at Pentecost reveals to them the true meaning of Jesus.

### If Time Allows (Step 1)
- **Scripture BLM: John 14:15–21** — Have children complete Scripture BLM 11 to gain a better understanding of the passage.

## STEP 2 — EXPLORE (pp. 90–92) — 20 minutes

### Page 90 — "A Wind from Heaven"

#### Student-facing content on p. 90

> In the Acts of the Apostles, we read about what Jesus' disciples did after his Ascension. Before that, Jesus had promised his disciples that they would receive the Holy Spirit. Now they were gathered in a house in Jerusalem with many others during a time of celebration. Suddenly the sound of a strong wind filled the house. Tongues of fire appeared over each person's head. Jesus' disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in different languages.
>
> Hearing the noise, a crowd of people gathered outside. They were from all over the world. The people in the crowd spoke many different languages. Yet each person heard the disciples' message in his or her own language. They were amazed that they all understood what the disciples were saying. This event marked the Feast of Pentecost.

Artwork on page: *Mary and Apostles at Pentecost*.

#### 1. Begin (catechist script)
- Find and play from the internet a video with the sound of wind in nature.
- **Say:** *"The disciples heard a strong wind when the Holy Spirit first visited them. They heard the wind inside the room where they were."*
- Tell children they will read a story told in the Bible — from the book of the **Acts of the Apostles**.

#### 2. Connect (scripted Q&A)

Read aloud the first paragraph. **Ask:**
- *"How does a breeze sound? How does a strong wind sound?"*
  - Expected: children may imitate a wind sound.

Explain that "tongues of fire" are small flames that represent the love of God. The flames above the disciples' heads were for God's love.

**Ask:** *"Who had come to the disciples?"*
- Expected: **the Holy Spirit**

Read aloud the second paragraph. **Ask:**
- *"Why were people outside both confused and amazed?"*
  - Expected: They all spoke different languages, but each person could understand everything the disciples said.

**Say:** *"The Holy Spirit made it possible for the disciples to reach all people with God's message."*

Have children point to the artwork. **Ask:**
- *"Who is in this painting?"* → Mary, the Apostles, disciples
- *"What is above their heads?"* → tongues of fire
- *"What do the flames symbolize?"* → the coming of the Holy Spirit

**Ask:** *"What does the expression 'fired up' mean?"*
- Expected: very enthusiastic

**Say:** *"The Holy Spirit got the disciples 'fired up' to tell the world about Jesus."*

#### If Time Allows (p. 90)
- **Activity BLM: Holy Spirit in Motion** — Children complete Activity BLM 11 to make a mobile of a fruit of the Holy Spirit.
- **Gatherings** — Provide poster board, magazines, scissors, and glue. Have children look for pictures of gatherings — people looking happy, surprised, and excited. Arrange them into collages titled "Gathering with Others."
- **QR Code:** Access a classroom activity about Pentecost.

### Page 91 — "The Disciples Accept Jesus' Mission" & "The Holy Spirit Builds the Church"

#### Student-facing content on p. 91

**The Disciples Accept Jesus' Mission**
> The disciples saw that the Holy Spirit had come to them, just as Jesus had promised. They were excited. The Holy Spirit gave them strength to continue Jesus' mission. The disciples were ready to tell people all over the world about Jesus. Each disciple would be a **witness** to Jesus.

**The Holy Spirit Builds the Church**
> At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit began to guide the Apostles as they formed their community around Jesus. Today the Holy Spirit works through us in filling the Church with life. Like the wind, we cannot see the Holy Spirit, but we feel its strength. The Holy Spirit helps us act as witnesses to Jesus' Death, Resurrection, and Ascension.

**Activity on page:** *Jesus' Mission in Our Lives* — Share your ideas about how you can continue Jesus' mission in your life. [Three blank lines for writing.]

**Link to Liturgy box:** The Feast of Pentecost is celebrated seven weeks after Easter Sunday.

#### Catechist script for p. 91

Read aloud the first heading on page 91. Write ***witness*** on the board and read aloud the paragraph.

**Ask:**
- *"What do witnesses do?"* → tell what they see or experience
- *"What does a witness to Jesus do?"* → believes in and tells other people about Jesus; continues Jesus' mission

**Say:** *"Every Christian has a duty to give witness to the Good News about Jesus, which is that he loves us and wants us to love God and one another."*

Point to the photos on the page. **Ask:**
- *"How are these children being witnesses to Jesus and carrying out his mission?"*
  - Possible answers: helping others, enjoying each other, caring

Read aloud the second heading and the paragraph. **Ask:**
- *"How did the Holy Spirit help people form the Church?"* → by giving disciples the strength to continue Jesus' mission
- *"How does the Holy Spirit continue to build the Church today?"* → by giving us the strength to be witnesses to Jesus and to spread God's Word

**Say:** *"The Holy Spirit is at work in us when we are excited about spreading God's love to others."*

**Activity:** Read aloud *Jesus' Mission in Our Lives* and discuss children's answers.

#### Link to Liturgy (catechist read-aloud)
Read aloud the feature. **Say:** *"On Pentecost Sunday, priests wear red vestments that symbolize the tongues of fire above the disciples' heads."*

#### 3. Close (for this step)
Use the following page and the Art Print easel to teach Art Print 11: *The Doves III*.

#### If Time Allows (p. 91)

Have children write messages such as "Jesus Loves You" and include images (like tongues of fire). You can provide cutouts to glue on. Have children bring their bookmarks home to share with a family member, or distribute to children in other grades.
  - Tagged as: **Solidarity**

### Page 92 — ART PRINT 11: *The Doves III*

#### Art Print introduction
Art Print 11 shows doves, which represent the presence of the Holy Spirit. Question for the children: *How do you feel the Holy Spirit's presence in your life?*

#### Student-facing content on p. 92

**The Holy Spirit Is with Us**
> Jesus sent the Holy Spirit to be with us and to guide us. The Holy Spirit helps us live prayerful lives and guides us on the journey to Heaven.
>
> Finish the sentence to show how you can be a witness to Jesus Christ.
>
> *"I can be a witness to Jesus Christ when I ___________________________."*

**Activity: Doves**
> The dove has been a religious symbol in art for more than 1,000 years. Its appearance can indicate peace, hope, forgiveness, inspiration, and the presence of the Holy Spirit. On the lines in the dove, write about a time in your life when you needed the Holy Spirit.

**Reading God's Word box:**
> "On coming up out of the water [Jesus] saw... the Spirit, like a dove, descending upon him." — Mark 1:10

#### Catechist script — Art Print 11

##### 1. Begin
Display the Art Print easel open to Art Print 11. Introduce the artwork using information from *About the Artist* and *Art•i•facts*.

**Ask:**
- *"What two similar things has the artist placed in the painting?"* → doves and lilies, or flowers
- *"What is interesting about the work?"* → The lilies appear to be taking flight as doves.

Explain that the doves in the painting suggest the Holy Spirit.

##### 2. Connect
Read aloud the introduction and discuss the question. Ask a volunteer to read aloud *The Holy Spirit Is with Us*.

**Ask:**
- *"Why did Jesus send the Holy Spirit to us?"* → to be with us and to guide us
- *"What does the Holy Spirit help us do?"* → live prayerful lives and know God's presence

Give children time to complete the sentence.

**Activity:** Read aloud *Doves*. **Ask:** *"What does a dove make us think of?"* → peace, reconciliation, the Holy Spirit. Give children time to write. Invite volunteers to share their work.

##### 3. Close (Art Print)
Invite children to reflect on the artwork and to pray a silent prayer thanking God for something that has filled them with wonder.

##### Reading God's Word (catechist script)
Invite a volunteer to read the passage. **Ask:**
- *"Why was Jesus coming out of the water?"* → He had just been baptized.
- *"Why is the Spirit compared with a dove?"* → It flies down lightly and is white, like light.

#### If Time Allows (p. 92)
- **Art Studio: Doves** — Have children create a drawing or painting of someone being blessed by a dove as they perform a kind act for someone else. Distribute paper and drawing/painting materials. Remind children to think of the dove as the Holy Spirit, inspiring us to be kind and loving to others.
- **Using the Art Print** — For additional activities and prayer opportunities, see the Art Print back.

## STEP 3 — REFLECT (pp. 93–94) — 15 minutes

### Page 93 — "Thankful for the Holy Spirit" / Children's Prayer

#### Student-facing content on p. 93

**Thankful for the Holy Spirit**
> Christians everywhere have prayed this prayer for more than 1,000 years. Invite the Holy Spirit into your heart. Now pray the prayer below.

**Prayer to the Holy Spirit** (traditional Catholic versicle-and-response)
> V. Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful.
> R. And kindle in them the fire of your love.
> V. Send forth your Spirit and they shall be created.
> R. And you shall renew the face of the earth.
>
> Let us pray:
> O God, by the light of the Holy Spirit you have taught the hearts of your faithful. In the same Spirit, help us to know what is truly right and always to rejoice in your consolation. We ask this through Christ, Our Lord.

#### Catechist script — Children's Prayer

**Prepare:** Pray the prayer in advance to become familiar with it.

Ask children to sit quietly and prepare to pray.

**Say:** *"The Holy Spirit helps us live prayerful lives. We invite the Holy Spirit to stay in our hearts always."*

Tell children that *consolation* means "comforting."

**Say:** *"Let us pray, asking the Holy Spirit to come to us."*

Read aloud the heading and the paragraph above the prayer. Tell children that in the first part of the prayer, "V" stands for *versicle*, the part read by the leader. Children should answer you by reading aloud the lines marked with "R," for *response*.

**Say:** *"We will all read the second part together."*

Pray the prayer together in this way, and conclude with *Amen*.

**Say:** *"Let's reflect quietly for a moment."*

Then lead children in praying the Sign of the Cross.

#### Sing with Movement (included at bottom of page)

**Say:** *"We will sing a song in which we invite the Holy Spirit into our hearts. It is called 'Come, O Holy Spirit / Wa Wa Wa Emimimo,' and it comes from Nigeria, a country in Africa. The song is sung in English and Yoruba, a language spoken in Nigeria."*

**Say:** *"We have invited the Holy Spirit into our hearts with this prayer. Let's allow the Holy Spirit to guide our actions every day."*

#### If Time Allows (p. 93)
- **Scripture Prayer Service BLM: John 14:15–21** — Optional prayer service focused on the opening Scripture; refer to Scripture Prayer Service BLM 11.
- **Sing with Movement** — In two groups, have children sing "Come, O Holy Spirit / Wa Wa Wa Emimimo." One group waves their arms during the words "wa wa wa" to represent the wind. The second group waves streamers to represent "tongues of fire." Have children repeat the song, one group singing English verses and one group singing in Yoruba, to represent the many different languages heard at Pentecost.

### Page 94 — "The Fruits and Gifts of the Holy Spirit"

#### Student-facing content on p. 94

> Just as the Holy Spirit came to the disciples at Pentecost, he also comes to us. The Holy Spirit helps us pray and serve God and others. When the Spirit guides our actions, we can see the results. These are the **Fruits of the Holy Spirit**:

**The 12 Fruits of the Holy Spirit (as listed in the book):**
1. love
2. joy
3. peace
4. patience
5. kindness
6. generosity
7. gentleness
8. faithfulness
9. self-control
10. goodness
11. modesty
12. chastity

**Activity: The Spirit in Our Actions**
> Read the list of Fruits of the Holy Spirit and think about their meanings. Then choose one of them. Write how it can be seen in your actions by finishing this sentence.
>
> *"The Fruit of ________ can be seen in my actions when I ________."*
>
> (Sample answer shown in book: The Fruit of **modesty** can be seen in my actions when I **don't seek praise and only want to serve God**.)

**Also introduced (but covered more deeply in the Pentecost feast pages):**
> Besides the 12 Fruits of the Holy Spirit, there are also **7 Gifts of the Holy Spirit**. They are: wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, and fear of the Lord. The Gifts of the Holy Spirit strengthen us and help us do what God the Father asks of us.

**Reading God's Word box:**
> "The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, generosity, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control." — Galatians 5:22–23

#### Catechist script — p. 94

##### 1. Begin
**Ask:** *"How can being patient help you?"*
- Possible answers: You do a better job; you show understanding of others' needs.

##### 2. Connect
Read aloud the heading and the first paragraph.

**Ask:**
- *"What happens when we let the Holy Spirit guide our actions?"* → We see the results of the Fruits of the Holy Spirit.
- *"What does it mean when we say that the Fruits of the Holy Spirit are seen in our actions?"* → When we cooperate with the Holy Spirit, one of the fruits is at work.

Ask volunteers to give examples of the Fruits.
- Example: Modesty helps me remember that what I have and what I am belong to God. Being modest, we also put into practice other qualities, such as patience and kindness.

**Activity:** Read aloud *The Spirit in Our Actions*. Give children time to complete the sentence. Invite them to share what they wrote.

##### 3. Close
Direct children's attention to the feature at the bottom of page 94 (Reading God's Word).

##### Reading God's Word (catechist script)
Read aloud the passage as children follow along. **Say:** *"This is from one of Paul's letters to his friends. He uses the Fruits of the Holy Spirit to list the qualities of people in a community guided by the Holy Spirit."*

#### If Time Allows (p. 94)
- **Fruits of the Holy Spirit Role-Play** — Arrange children into small groups and assign each a fruit of the Holy Spirit. Have them create short skits illustrating actions guided by the Holy Spirit that result in their group's fruit. Each group presents.

#### Inclusion note: Social Coordination
Because the complexity of social interaction grows with the size of a small group, offer options for dyads and triads, and don't allow a group to be any bigger than four members. Children aren't always aware of the number with which they can successfully coordinate — you may need to assign groupings based on your knowledge of students' social coordination skills.

## PENTECOST — ENGAGE (p. 233)

### Student-facing content on p. 233

**Pentecost**
> Before he ascended into heaven, Jesus promised to send the Holy Spirit to the disciples. On Pentecost, the Holy Spirit came to the disciples, or Apostles, and Mary. Pentecost is often associated with the beginning of the Church.
>
> The word *Pentecost* comes from a Greek word that means "fiftieth." We celebrate Pentecost on the seventh Sunday, or the 50th day, after Easter.
>
> The Spirit strengthens us to continue Jesus' mission and calls us to share our gifts with the Church and community. We know that our talents and abilities are God's gifts to us. We pray that God will show us how to use those gifts.

**Prayer (student):**
> Dear Jesus, help me to use my gifts. I want to live as your disciple and continue your work.

### Catechist script — p. 233

#### 1. Set the Stage
**Say:** *"Imagine you've received a gift that inspires you to go out and do something."* Invite children to describe what that might be.

**Say:** *"Ten days after his Ascension — that is, 50 days after Easter — Jesus sent the Apostles a gift: the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit strengthened them and inspired them to form the Church in order to spread throughout the world the Good News of Jesus' Death, Resurrection, and Ascension."*

#### 2. Get Started
Read aloud the session title and invite volunteers to read aloud the paragraphs.

**Ask:**
- *"What do we celebrate on Pentecost?"* → the Holy Spirit coming to the Apostles

**Say:** *"The liturgical color of Pentecost is red, a color of life and excitement."*

**Ask:**
- *"What does the Holy Spirit call us to do?"* → share our gifts with the Church and community

#### Prayer
Prepare children for prayer. **Say:** *"Pray with me in the quiet of your hearts while I pray aloud."* Pray the prayer aloud. Allow quiet time for children to add their own intentions. Then pray *Amen*.

## PENTECOST — EXPLORE (pp. 234–235)

### Page 234 — "One in the Spirit"

#### Student-facing content on p. 234

> Paul worked to help followers of Christ understand how the Holy Spirit works in each of us. He expressed his ideas in a letter to his friends in Corinth.

**Scripture (1 Corinthians 12:4–11):**
> There are different kinds of spiritual gifts but the same Spirit; there are different forms of service but the same Lord; there are different workings but the same God who produces all of them in everyone. To each individual the manifestation of the Spirit is given for some benefit. To one is given through the Spirit the expression of wisdom; to another the expression of knowledge according to the same Spirit; to another faith by the same Spirit; to another gifts of healing by the one Spirit; to another mighty deeds; to another prophecy; to another discernment of spirits; to another varieties of tongues; to another interpretation of tongues. But one and the same Spirit produces all of these, distributing them individually to each person as he wishes.

**Reflection prompt on page:**
> Paul tells us that each of us has our own gifts from God. Our gifts are ways the Holy Spirit shines through us. What are your gifts? How do you use them?

#### Catechist script — p. 234

##### 1. Begin
**Say:** *"We have met Saint Paul and know how much he liked to write letters to the people of the churches he visited. We are going to read from a letter Paul sent to Jesus' followers in Corinth."* Remind children that Saint Paul's letters are meant for us to read too and that we can find them in the New Testament.

##### 2. Connect
Invite volunteers to read aloud the heading, the first paragraph, and the Scripture passage.

**Say:** *"Paul points out that we all have different qualities, but they all come from the same source — the Spirit. They are all different ways to serve the same God."*

**Ask:**
- *"What are some ways a person is unique, meaning different from others?"* → appearance, preferences, abilities, manner, language
- *"Why do you think Paul wrote this?"* → Sometimes we want everyone to be like us, and we must remember that God made everyone different.

Read aloud the first two sentences of the last paragraph. Pause before the questions at the end.

**Say:** *"Close your eyes and reflect on each question as I ask it. See what comes to mind. Be aware if your mind responds with pictures or images. Think about what you see yourself doing."*

#### FYI: Discernment of Spirits (catechist background)

# QUICK REFERENCE — KEY FACTS & CHEAT SHEET

## The story of Pentecost (one-paragraph version for kids)
After Jesus returned to Heaven (the Ascension), his disciples — including Mary and the Apostles — were gathered together in a house in Jerusalem. Suddenly they heard the sound of a strong wind filling the house, and small flames like "tongues of fire" appeared over each person's head. The Holy Spirit filled them, and they began speaking in many different languages. A crowd from all over the world gathered outside, and each person heard the message in his or her own language. This is Pentecost — the day the Holy Spirit came and the Church was born.

## Key dates / liturgical facts
- Pentecost = 50th day after Easter (the seventh Sunday after Easter)
- The word *Pentecost* comes from Greek for "fiftieth"
- Liturgical color: **red** (for life, excitement, and the tongues of fire)
- Priests wear red vestments on Pentecost Sunday

## The 12 Fruits of the Holy Spirit (from Session 11, p. 94)
love • joy • peace • patience • kindness • generosity • gentleness • faithfulness • self-control • goodness • modesty • chastity

(Note: Galatians 5:22–23 in the NABRE lists nine: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, generosity, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. The Catholic tradition adds three more — goodness, modesty, chastity — drawn from the Vulgate tradition, giving twelve. The student page uses the traditional twelve.)

## The 7 Gifts of the Holy Spirit (from Pentecost session, p. 235)
wisdom • understanding • counsel • fortitude • knowledge • piety • fear of the Lord

- **witness** — someone who tells others about Jesus

## Likely spots where 3rd graders may get confused
- The distinction between **Fruits** (12, the results we see) and **Gifts** (7, what the Spirit gives us to empower us). Be ready to restate simply: *Gifts are what the Holy Spirit gives us; Fruits are what we show to the world when we use those gifts.*
- The word "Advocate" — plan a simple definition: "someone who stands up for you."
- "Tongues of fire" — make clear these are not real flames but a symbol of God's love.
