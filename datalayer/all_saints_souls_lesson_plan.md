**Big idea: We are not alone. Everyone who loves God — in heaven, in Purgatory, and here on earth — is one family: the Communion of Saints.**

**Our definition (say it together at least three times today):**
> **"A saint is someone who loves God and loves other people, and lets God help them do it."**

Three parts, three hand motions: **loves God** (point up) · **loves other people** (arms out wide) · **lets God help them do it** (hands open, palms up). The third part is the book's point about grace: "The Holy Spirit gives us the grace to do so" (p. 238). Saints aren't people who do it all on their own.

Outcomes (from the Catechist Guide, p. 237):

* All Saints Day and All Souls Day celebrate the **Communion of Saints**.
* In Christ, we are united with the living and the dead.
* The saints show us how to live our lives.

**Before class (5 minutes)**

* Write on the board: **ALL HALLOWS' EVE** → HALLOWEEN. Under it: **Nov 1 = All Saints** · **Nov 2 = All Souls**.
* Also on the board, big: **A saint is someone who loves God and loves other people, and lets God help them do it.**
* Prayer table: Bible open to **1 Corinthians 12:12–13**. Add a statue or picture of Mary if one is in the room.
* Optional: small heart stickers (the book uses them for the send-off). A marker heart on the hand also works.
* Have the Christmas play scripts ready to hand out at the end.
* Donuts: hand them out while the 4th graders present, or at the very end. Not during prayer.

**Scripture for today (quick reference)**

*1 John 4:11–13* (book wording, p. 238)
> Beloved, if God so loved us, we also must love one another. No one has ever seen God. Yet, if we love one another, God remains in us, and his love is brought to perfection in us. This is how we know that we remain in him and he in us, that he has given us of his Spirit.

*Why we read it:* When we love other people, God's love grows in us. That is how ordinary people become saints.

*1 Corinthians 12:12–13* (book wording, p. 240 prayer service)
> As a body is one though it has many parts, and all the parts of the body, though many, are one body, so also Christ. For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, slaves or free persons, and we were all given to drink of one Spirit.

*Why we read it:* We are all one body in Jesus. That body includes the saints in heaven, the souls in Purgatory, and us. That is the Communion of Saints.

*Luke 1:28 and 1:42* (King James; where the Hail Mary comes from, block 5)
> 28 And the angel came in unto her, and said, Hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women.
>
> 42 And she [Elizabeth] spake out with a loud voice, and said, Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb.

*Why we read it:* The first half of the Hail Mary uses these exact Bible words. The angel and Elizabeth praised Mary first; we join them.

### The 30 minutes

The 4th graders arrive at a time we don't control. **When they knock, stop where you are, let them present, then pick up at the next block.** If they take a long time, use the cut list below, in order.

**1 · Halloween hook + prayer (0–4 min)** — p. 237

* Point to the board. Ask: **Who went trick-or-treating last night?** (Let a few hands go up. Keep it short.)
* Say: **Halloween is a church word! "Hallowed" means holy — like "hallowed be thy name" in the Our Father.** Ask: **So who are the "hallows"?** (the holy people — the saints)
* Say: **October 31 is All Hallows' Eve — the night before All Saints Day. "All Hallows' Eve" got squished into "Halloween."**
* Ask: **So what IS a saint?** Take two or three guesses. Then point to the board and read it together, with the hand motions: **"A saint is someone who loves God and loves other people, and lets God help them do it."** (1st time)
* Prayer. Say: **Let us ask Jesus to help us serve God and others like the saints did.** Pause, then pray together:
  > *Jesus, let me be united with you and with all the angels and saints, and with my brothers and sisters. Amen.*

**2 · Two feast days (4–9 min)** — p. 237

* Volunteers read the title and the two paragraphs on p. 237.
* Make the difference clear (point to the board):
  * **Nov 1, All Saints:** the holy people who are with God in heaven now. We ask them to pray for us.
  * **Nov 2, All Souls:** people who have died who are **not yet fully ready** for heaven. We pray for them.
* **Purgatory in kid words** (from p. 239): **Sin puts a little distance between us and God. Some people die before all that distance is gone. Purgatory is where God cleans it away, so they can see Him face to face in heaven. Our prayers help them.**
* **God cleans us here, too — sorry at Mass vs. Confession.** Say:
  * **At the start of Mass, we all say sorry to God together ("Lord, have mercy"). That's like washing your hands before dinner. It's for our everyday mistakes.**
  * **Confession is like a full bath. It cleans everything, even the big stuff, and God gives us extra strength to be good.**
  * **God ALWAYS wants to forgive us. Confession is a gift.** (You'll make your first Confession in February.)
  * *Catechist note:* keep "big stuff" vague. Don't list examples. The goal is that no one leaves worried.
* Ask: **Is there someone in your family who has died that you want to pray for this week?** (Just a few, or have them think it silently. Some kids may have a recent loss. Keep it gentle.)

**3 · Like Saints + the Communion of Saints (9–15 min)** — p. 238

* Ask: **Tell me a time you helped someone.** (helping a little brother tie his shoe, talking to a lonely neighbor) Say: **When we serve others, God's love grows in us.**
* **The four portraits.** Have kids point to each one as you read the name. One line each (catechist background, not in the student book):
  * **St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross** (Edith Stein) — grew up Jewish in Germany, became a Catholic and a nun. She was killed by the Nazis in 1942.
  * **Venerable Pierre Toussaint** — born enslaved in Haiti. He became a hairdresser in New York and used his money to care for orphans, the sick and the poor.
  * **St. Thérèse of Lisieux** ("the Little Flower") — a French nun who died at 24. Her "Little Way": do small, ordinary things with great love. (We come back to her at the end.)
  * **Blessed Carlos Rodríguez** ("Charlie") — from Puerto Rico. He was often sick, but spent his life helping people love the Mass and Easter.
  * Say: **These four lived in the last 200 years. Saints aren't only from long, long ago. Some of them looked like regular people you'd meet today.**
  * Check them against our definition. Pick one (Pierre Toussaint works well) and ask: **How did he love God? How did he love other people? Could he do all that on his own?** (He went to Mass every day; he cared for orphans and the sick; God helped him.)
* Volunteer reads the first paragraph + **1 John 4:11–13**. Ask: **What happens when we love one another?** (God's love grows in us and in others.)
* Point out the last line, "he has given us of his Spirit." Say: **That's the "lets God help" part. The Holy Spirit helps us love.** Say the definition together. (2nd time)
* Volunteer reads **The Communion of Saints**. Ask: **Where else do we hear the word "communion"?** (First Communion / the Eucharist) Say: **Communion means coming together as one.** Ask: **Who is in the Communion of Saints?** (everyone, living and dead, who loves God)
* **Say this one twice: "The Communion of Saints isn't just the saints in heaven. It includes you, me, and everyone who follows Jesus."**

**4 · Saints weren't perfect (15–19 min)** — ties to "lets God help them do it"

* Say: **Here's a secret: saints weren't perfect. They made mistakes, just like us. What made them saints is that they kept letting God help them.**
* **St. Augustine and the pears.** **When Augustine was a teenager, he and his friends stole pears from a neighbor's tree. He didn't even want the pears! He did it just because his friends were doing it.** Ask: **Ever done something just because your friends were doing it?** (Hands up is enough. No stories needed.)
* **St. Monica, his mom.** **Augustine's mom, Monica, prayed for him for years and years. God answered her prayers. Augustine became one of the greatest saints ever.** Say: **Our prayers for other people matter! That's the Communion of Saints at work.** Their feast days are side by side: **Monica Aug 27, Augustine Aug 28.**
* **St. Mark Ji Tianxiang** (China, 1800s). **Mark got very sick. He took a medicine, and his body got stuck needing it. He couldn't stop. But for 30 years he kept going to church and never gave up on God.** Say: **He kept letting God help him, even when it was really hard.**
  * *Catechist note:* don't use the word "addiction" with this age. If a child asks, "his body got stuck needing the medicine" is enough. (Background: it was opium. He died for his faith in 1900 and was canonized in 2000.)
* The **"A Saint in the Making"** story on p. 239 goes home with the page. No time to write it today.

**5 · Prayer service (19–24 min)** — p. 240

* Gather at the prayer table. Say: **As we pray, we look to Mary, the greatest saint. She is sometimes called "Queen of All Saints."**
* Pick one volunteer as **Reader**. You are **Leader**. Point out the **All** lines. Pray the service on p. 240.
* **Add a short litany (optional, 1 minute):** go around the room. Each child says the name of a saint (their patron saint or a 4th-grade presentation saint), and the class answers **"pray for us."** Then: **"For everyone in our families who has died —"** All: **"Lord, give them rest."** (Kids may say a name out loud or silently.)
* **Before the Hail Mary, explain its two halves:**
  * **First half praises Mary.** The words come from the Bible: the angel Gabriel ("Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you") and her cousin Elizabeth ("Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb").
  * **Second half ("Holy Mary, Mother of God…") asks her to pray for us.**
  * Kid line: **"The first part says nice things to Mary, and the second part asks her to pray for us."**
* End with the **Hail Mary** together.

**6 · God wants YOU to be a saint + go in peace (24–30 min)** — pp. 239–240

* Say: **Being a saint isn't just for priests and nuns. God wants every one of YOU to be a saint.**
* **St. Thérèse's Little Way** (point to her on p. 238): **She didn't do giant things. She did small things with great love — being kind to someone who annoyed her, doing a chore without complaining.**
* **St. Carlo Acutis:** **Carlo was a regular teenager. He loved video games, soccer and computers. He used his computer skills to teach people about Jesus in the Eucharist. He died at 15, and in 2025 the Church named him a saint.** Say: **A kid who played video games is a saint! So you can be one too.**
* **Go around the room:** **"What is ONE small thing you will do this week to be a saint?"** Each child answers in one sentence. (The book's close on p. 239: "We are all saints whenever we treat someone in a way that shows that we know God loves them.")
* **Respond (quick):** listen for: what a saint is (our definition) · Halloween = All Hallows' Eve · Nov 1 saints in heaven / Nov 2 souls in Purgatory · Communion of Saints = all of us together in Jesus · saints weren't perfect · our prayers for others matter.
* Say: **These feasts remind us that we are never alone. We are part of the Communion of Saints!**
* Last time, with the hand motions: **"A saint is someone who loves God and loves other people, and lets God help them do it."** (3rd time) Say: **You can be a saint too. Start today.**
* Heart sticker (or marker heart) on each hand. Say: **When you look at your heart, remember the saints. Try to love and serve someone today like they did.**
* **Tear out pp. 237–240 and send them home** (standing syllabus instruction).
* Hand out **Christmas Pageant scripts**. Donuts if not yet eaten. Line up for play practice.

### Coloring page: "Saints Like Me"

The four saints from p. 238 in round frames around a heart, plus an empty frame where each child draws themselves with a halo. It ties to the line "the Communion of Saints includes you." Use it as the take-home, or while the 4th graders set up. Other concepts offered: "One Family in Jesus" (heaven / earth / candles for loved ones), "Halloween → All Saints" (split page).

Gemini prompt:

```
Create a printable black-and-white coloring page for 8-year-old children, portrait orientation, US Letter size.

Style: clean line art only. Thick, smooth black outlines on a pure white background. No shading, no gray, no color, no hatching. Large simple shapes with plenty of open space to color. Friendly, gentle cartoon style suitable for a Catholic religious education class.

Layout:
- At the top, a big bubble-letter title that reads exactly: SAINTS LIKE ME
- In the center, a large heart. Inside the heart, in simple block letters, exactly: LOVE ONE ANOTHER. Under the heart, small text exactly: 1 John 4:11
- Around the heart, four round portrait frames (like medallions), each with a simple halo above the head and a banner ribbon under the frame with the name:
  1. Top left: a smiling woman in a simple nun's veil, holding a small cross. Banner: ST. TERESA BENEDICTA
  2. Top right: a kind Black man in early-1800s clothing (high collar, cravat) holding a loaf of bread to share. Banner: PIERRE TOUSSAINT
  3. Bottom left: a young nun in a Carmelite habit holding a bunch of roses. Banner: ST. THERESE
  4. Bottom right: a cheerful young man in a 1950s suit and round glasses holding a small Easter candle. Banner: CARLOS RODRIGUEZ
- At the bottom center, a fifth round frame that is EMPTY inside except for a simple halo outline at the top, with a banner that reads exactly: ME!  Next to it, a small line of text: Draw yourself as a saint!
- Fill the remaining background lightly with simple stars and small hearts to color. Keep it uncluttered.

Spell every word exactly as written above. No other text.
```

Fixes if Gemini misfires: if the text comes out garbled, ask it to "regenerate with no text at all" and hand-letter the title. If the portraits get too detailed, add "simpler faces, fewer lines, like a children's coloring book."
