## Step 3: Build the Lesson Plan

Create a detailed, minute-by-minute lesson plan for a **1-hour class** with three parts:

### Part A: Bible Search Race (~10 minutes)

Design a timed, competitive, collaborative Bible search activity using the Session 20 scripture references:

- **John 4:5–15** (Jesus and the Samaritan woman)
- **Matthew 25:40** ("Whatever you did for one of these least brothers of mine...")
- **Luke 21:1–4** (The poor widow's offering)
- **Tobit 4:7** ("Give alms from your possessions...")

Structure this as **4 rounds** — one verse per round. For each round, give me:
- The exact verse reference to call out
- A short setup line I say before they search (one sentence to hook them)
- What the kids do once they find it (read aloud, answer a specific question about it, etc.)
- How to score/reward (team points, first-to-find, etc.)

Split the class into 2–3 teams. Keep rules dead simple. Every kid should have a Bible or share one.

### Part B: Core Lesson — Lent, Almsgiving, Fasting, Prayer (~25–30 minutes)

Cover the main content from the YAML (engage, explore, reflect sections). Adapt it for maximum engagement using these methods:

- **Read-aloud together** — have them read passages as a group or take turns
- **Storytelling** — I tell the Samaritan woman story and the widow's offering story in my own words; give me a short, vivid retelling script for each
- **Directed questions only** — NO open-ended questions. Use these formats instead:
  - "Raise your hand if..." (yes/no)
  - "Was it A or B?" (either/or)
  - Fill-in-the-blank (I say a sentence, they shout the missing word)
  - "True or false?"
  - "How many..." (number answer)
- **Mini-competitions** within the lesson (e.g., vocabulary speed round, team challenges)
- **The intercession activity** — each kid writes one intercession, then we go around the room and say "Lord, hear our prayer" after each one. Make this feel like a real moment, not busywork.

For each segment, give me:
- Approximate time
- Exactly what I say and do
- Exactly what the kids do
- Transition to the next segment (keep it fast — dead time = lost kids)

### Part C: Jeopardy Game (~15–20 minutes)

Build a complete, ready-to-use Jeopardy game with:

- **5 categories**, each with questions at 100, 200, 300, 400, 500 points
- **~50% Session 20 content**: Lenten vocabulary (almsgiving, intercessions, fasting, Prayer of the Faithful), scripture stories, practices
- **~50% year review content**: drawn from the themes I provide in Step 2
- For every question, provide the **answer** as well
- Format it so I can project it, write categories on the board, or just read questions aloud
- Include a **Final Jeopardy** question that ties the year together

Teams carry over from the Bible Search. Keep scoring simple (I can track on the board).

## Teaching Philosophy — Follow These Rules

These are non-negotiable constraints for the entire lesson plan:

1. **No open-ended questions.** Kids derail them every time. Use directed questions with concrete answers (either/or, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, raise-your-hand, number answers).
2. **Competition works.** Teams, points, winners. Every activity should have stakes.
3. **Reading and working together works.** Group reading, partner work, class responses.
4. **Storytelling works.** When I tell the story in my own words, they listen. Give me short scripts.
5. **Fast transitions.** Every activity needs a clear start signal, clear end signal, and an immediate bridge to the next thing. No dead time.
6. **Clear structure.** Every activity has: what I say to start it, what the kids do, how it ends, who wins.
7. **Assume resistance.** These kids are not naturally excited to be here. Every minute needs to earn their attention.
8. **1 hour total.** Budget time realistically. Better to have a tight plan than an ambitious one that runs over.
